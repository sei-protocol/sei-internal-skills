# Attested-channel (RA-TLS / attested transport) technique kit

> Ground truth: the RA-TLS paper, the IETF RATS RFCs + drafts, and the reference-implementation docs cited inline below (plus the `tee-profile.md` overlay and the sibling attester kits) — this kit is a **self-contained kernel** that stands alone on those **public** sources. Cite the primary source; do not paraphrase. No dependency on any private/internal research archive.
>
> **This kit is NOT an attester platform kit, and NOT the on-chain Verifier kit.** It describes a **cross-cutting technique** — how a freshly-generated **transport key is content-bound to a hardware attestation** so a TLS / secure channel is trusted **because of the measurement running at the far end, not because a CA vouched for a name**. It produces no Evidence of its own and holds no measurement registers; it **inherits** the trust set of whichever attester platform produces the Evidence it carries. Where the §1–§7 template is shaped for an Attester, this kit adapts the section and says why (as `kit-sei-onchain.md` does for the Verifier layer). Load the matching attester kit (`kit-aws-nitro.md`, `kit-intel-sgx-tdx.md`, `kit-amd-sev-snp.md`, `kit-nvidia-cc.md`) alongside this one per method Step 3.

## 1. Identity & RATS roles

This technique is the **secure-channel carrier + the handshake-time appraisal pattern** — not the Attester, not the on-chain Verifier.

- **What it is** — a freshly-generated transport keypair (the TLS/session key) whose public half is **content-bound into the attester's signed report-data**, so verifying the channel proves the peer holds the channel key *inside* a genuine TEE boundary — and, **once the verifier also enforces measurement-membership (§3 / VP2)**, that it is the *expected* measured TEE. **The binding alone proves key-in-enclave; VP2 is what proves *which* enclave** — keep the two distinct (§3). The canonical instantiation is **RA-TLS** (Intel SGX; Knauth et al., arXiv:1801.05863) / **aTLS** (the same pattern on VM-TEEs). It performs attestation **during connection setup, without changing the TLS protocol** (RA-TLS embeds the quote in an X.509 extension; the IETF in-handshake route is still pre-standardization — §2).
- **RATS role mapping** — the **verifying peer** acts as **Verifier + Relying Party** in the **background-check** topology (it receives raw Evidence in the handshake and appraises it inline), or it consumes a pre-issued **Attestation Result** in the **passport** topology (§3 of method; RFC 9334). **Attester** = the in-scope TEE (its kit). **Endorser** = that vendor's PKI (attester kit §1). **Reference-Value-Provider** = governance / the on-chain registry (`tee-profile.md` §3).
- **Trust root / Endorser — there is NO single trust root for this technique.** It **inherits** the attester kit's trust set, and crucially it **expands** the relying party's trust set versus plain TLS: the RP now additionally trusts (a) the silicon vendor's RoT + signing chain, (b) frequently the cloud hypervisor/host on VM-TEEs, and (c) the attestation/verification service (RFC 9334 trust anchors). That delta has a **live forged-attestation CVE history** — BadRAM / CVE-2024-21944 (AMD-SB-3015) forges valid SEV-SNP reports with <$10 of hardware (badram.eu); see VP16.

**Template-fit note:** the template's §1 "Endorser / trust root to pin" framing is Attester-shaped. For this technique the trust root is *inherited, plural, and per-peer* (captured above + VP16). The technique's own correctness rests not on a pinned silicon root but on the **verifier-policy gates in §4** — as the IETF draft states plainly, attested TLS as a bare protocol "only guarantees that the Identity Key is known by the TEE" (`draft-fossati-tls-attestation-09` §10.1); everything else is policy layered on top.

**What this technique does NOT give you (negative space — method "Trust-model-honesty discipline").** An attested channel does **not** make a *buggy or runtime-compromised but correctly-measured* enclave trustworthy — attestation proves the *bytes are the approved build*, not that the build is correct; a memory-safety bug or logic flaw in the approved measurement is fully in scope and fully exploitable *over a perfectly attested channel*. It does **not** provide confidentiality against the host on a host-trusting platform (validator-as-host — §6, `tee-profile.md` §1–§2). It does **not** survive a reference-value-registry compromise (VP15). It adds **no** TLS downgrade resistance of its own (VP13). And a *valid binding says nothing about debug state* (VP3) — see VP6.

## 2. Evidence format — what the channel carries

The technique ships the attester's **vendor-native Evidence** over the handshake via one of two carriers, plus the **key-binding slot** that makes the channel non-relayable.

- **Carrier A — RA-TLS / aTLS X.509 certificate extension (deployed norm).** The enclave generates an ephemeral keypair inside the TEE, builds a self-signed cert, and embeds the quote as a **custom X.509 v3 extension**; the standard TLS 1.3 handshake then proceeds unchanged and the peer extracts + verifies the quote in a custom certificate-verify callback. Modern Gramine uses the **TCG DICE "tagged evidence" OID** with CBOR-encoded claims including a `pubkey-hash` claim (and emits a legacy non-standard OID for back-compat) (Gramine *Attestation*; Knauth et al.). *The literal legacy OID numeric string is unverified — no public primary source re-checked here.*
- **Carrier B — in-handshake attestation messages (IETF, pre-standardization).** `draft-fossati-tls-attestation-09` (Apr 2025, **expired, never WG-adopted**) defined four TLS 1.3 extensions (`evidence_proposal`/`evidence_request` for background-check, `results_proposal`/`results_request` for passport) carrying credentials in the Certificate message bound to a **TLS Identity Key (TIK)**. Its **active successor**, `draft-fossati-seat-early-attestation-04` (May 2026, expires Nov 2026, **individual / not WG-adopted**), **replaces the cert-extension with a dedicated CMW-carrying "Attestation" handshake message**. **Bottom line for 2026: attestation-in-the-handshake is still pre-WG** — design against Carrier A (cert extension) and treat Carrier B as emerging.
- **The key-binding slot (the load-bearing primitive).** The hash of the channel public key goes into the attester's signed report-data field: SGX/TDX `REPORTDATA` (64 B), SEV-SNP `REPORT_DATA` (64 B), or the AWS Nitro COSE document's `public_key` field — field detail in each attester kit §2/§3. **EAT (RFC 9711)** is the passport-model *result-token* format, not an on-chain input (method "Note on output format"; `kit-sei-onchain.md` §2).

**Template-fit note:** §2 normally describes *the wire format an Attester produces*. Here it is reframed as *the two carriers the channel uses to transport that Evidence + the public-key-binding slot it writes into* — the per-vendor wire format itself is redirected to the attester kits' §2.

## 3. Identity & measurement fields (VP2) — inherited, plus the key↔measurement bind

**N/A as identity-measurement.** This technique produces no measurement registers of its own. For binary-identity field semantics, redirect to the attester kits: SGX MRENCLAVE/MRSIGNER/ISV_PROD_ID/ISV_SVN (`kit-intel-sgx-tdx.md` §3); Nitro PCR0/1/2 (`kit-aws-nitro.md` §3); TDX `MRTD`+`RTMR[0..3]` together — MRTD-alone is exploitable (`kit-intel-sgx-tdx.md` §3); AMD `MEASUREMENT` (`kit-amd-sev-snp.md` §3).

What this technique **owns** is the **dual bind** that distinguishes a relayed channel from a genuine one:
1. **measurement membership** — the parsed measurement MUST match the reference-value set (VP2), and
2. **key binding** — the verifier recomputes the hash of the presented certificate's public key and **requires equality with the value the hardware signed into report-data** (Knauth et al.; Gramine *Attestation*). Skipping (2) reopens the relay/MITM (§4 VP1/VP6); skipping (1) admits "*an* enclave" instead of "*the expected* enclave" (the crux failure, VP2).

The **bound value is per-vendor**: SGX/TDX/AMD bind `hash(pubkey)` into the 64-B report-data channel; **AWS Nitro binds the raw public-key bytes in the `public_key` field** (not a hash), and on the KMS-release path that key must be **RSA** (§6). An implementer must read the matching attester kit for which-hash / which-key (`kit-aws-nitro.md` §6 for the RSA constraint).

**Template-fit note:** the template's §3 "what proves binary identity, the registers, required-together rule" is intrinsically an Attester property — redirected to the attester kits — and §3 is **repurposed** to the one identity-adjacent thing the channel owns: the public-key↔measurement binding. Deliberate adaptation, not a gap.

## 4. Verifier-policy specifics — what the CHANNEL verifier enforces

A dimension is **ENFORCED here** when the channel-establishing verifier performs the check at handshake time; **attester-specific** when the per-vendor field/bit lives in the attester kit and the channel only consumes it; **profile** when it is a registry/governance concern. IDs verbatim per `method.md`. A miss in any gate collapses the §1 guarantee — treat each as a separate required check.

For **mutual** aTLS the nonce is **bidirectional** (each side's nonce in the other's report-data — VP8). ## 5. On-chain verification (Sei) — relationship to the on-chain layer

**N/A as a direct on-chain cost.** The attested channel is an **off-chain transport technique**; it adds no Sei-EVM verification path of its own. Its relationship to the on-chain layer is the point:

- The attested channel is the **off-chain complement** to `kit-sei-onchain.md`'s registered-key amortization. The channel between an enclave and an off-chain relying party (a relayer, a key broker, a peer enclave) is what carries the **secp256k1 binding key / witness statements** that the on-chain Verifier later accepts at ~3k gas (`kit-sei-onchain.md` §5–§6) — establishing that key over a *measurement-verified* channel is what makes the amortization trustworthy. **Do not reuse that binding key on-chain without the witness-key-freshness controls `kit-sei-onchain.md` §5–§6 mandates** (per-message sequence numbers, rotation on TCB/image change, an on-chain validity window) — a key minted over a clean aTLS channel still outlives its attestation without them.
- The **measurement allow-list the channel enforces (VP2) is the same reference-value set the on-chain registry governs** as RATS Reference-Value-Provider (`tee-profile.md` §3) — one governed set, enforced both off-chain at the channel and on-chain at the registry.
- On **AWS Nitro**, the **KMS attestation-gated key-release substitutes for an attested transport entirely**: KMS is the relying party, ingests the attestation document, and returns `CiphertextForRecipient` encrypted to the document's `public_key` — no RA-TLS channel needed for the secret-release case (§6; `kit-aws-nitro.md` §6).

**Template-fit note:** §5 normally gives the per-vendor Sei-EVM cost/path. This technique has none; §5 is **repurposed** to its composition with the on-chain Verifier kit and the shared governed reference-value set.

## 6. Integration pattern (the technique's core) — RA-TLS / mutual attested mTLS, per platform

The split is **library-first (SGX process enclaves)** vs **assembly-first (VM-TEEs)**.

**SGX — turnkey RA-TLS (Gramine / Open Enclave / EGo+MarbleRun).**
- **Gramine**: `ra_tls_attest.so` on the attester side; `ra_tls_verify_dcap.so` on the verifier side (EPID is end-of-life — use **DCAP**, manifest `sgx.remote_attestation = "dcap"`). APIs: `ra_tls_create_key_and_crt_der()` (generate in-enclave keypair + DER cert with embedded quote, REPORTDATA = hash of cert pubkey), `ra_tls_verify_callback_der()` (plug into the mbedTLS/OpenSSL verify callback), `ra_tls_set_measurement_callback()` (accept/reject MRENCLAVE/MRSIGNER/ISV_PROD_ID/ISV_SVN). Secret Provisioning (`secret_prov_*`) layers attested key-release on top (Gramine *Attestation*).
- **Open Enclave**: `oe_get_attestation_certificate_with_evidence_v2()` / `oe_verify_attestation_certificate_with_evidence[_v2]()` — same cert-with-evidence-extension pattern, SGX-FLC only (OE attested_tls README).
- **EGo + MarbleRun** — the **scaling pattern**: a Marble generates an EC key + self-signed cert, quotes over the cert hash, and aTLS-joins a central **Coordinator** that verifies measurements against a Manifest and issues CA-signed creds; **runtime mTLS between Marbles then chains to the Coordinator CA with no further quotes** ("transparent TLS") — attest once at join, ordinary mTLS thereafter (MarbleRun *Attestation* / *Transparent TLS*). **Federation caveat:** transparent TLS trades per-session freshness for scale — post-join, peers trust the *Coordinator CA, not a live measurement*, so a **runtime-compromised-but-approved** Marble (the buggy-enclave case, §1 negative space) keeps a valid cert and is trusted with **no re-attestation**, and the **Coordinator is a single point whose compromise mints federation-wide trust**; pair with VP7's re-attestation interval. *The Marble's exact key curve / quote-hash and EGo's Go aTLS function names are unverified — no primary doc fetched this pass.*

**AWS Nitro — assembly-first (no RA-TLS library).**
- The COSE_Sign1 **envelope** is signed **P-384 / ES384 / SHA-384 (COSE tag 18)** (`kit-aws-nitro.md` §2): a verifier rebuilds the `Sig_structure` (context `"Signature1"`), converts the 96-byte raw `r‖s` to DER, verifies against the embedded cert, and PKIX-validates the reversed `cabundle` to the **AWS Nitro Enclaves root CA (G1)** (AWS *Validating attestation documents*; nsm-api `attestation_process.md`). The channel key is bound in the document's **separate `public_key` field** — and for the KMS-release path that key **must be RSA (`RSAES_OAEP_SHA_256`); an EC key there is rejected** (`kit-aws-nitro.md` §6). (Do not conflate the two: P-384 is the *envelope signature*, not the bound `public_key`. For a direct-verify channel where KMS is not the relying party, the binding may be a hash and the RSA constraint does not apply.) *Re-pull the live G1 root fingerprint from AWS before it goes load-bearing.*
- **Dominant pattern — delegate to KMS**: pass the document via the KMS `Recipient` parameter; KMS verifies PCRs against the key policy and returns **`CiphertextForRecipient`** encrypted to the RSA `public_key`, gated by **`kms:RecipientAttestation:ImageSha384`** (== PCR0) and the per-register `:PCR<n>` condition keys over Decrypt / GenerateDataKey[Pair] / GenerateRandom / DeriveSharedSecret (AWS KMS *How Nitro Enclaves uses KMS*; AWS Prescriptive Guidance). vsock-only; the parent proxies and never sees plaintext (`kit-aws-nitro.md` §6). **Trust-model caveat (`tee-profile.md` §2):** KMS attestation-gated release defends a relying party who is **NOT** the AWS host; against a relying party who **IS** the host (validator-as-host) it gates the host's own enclave with the host's own KMS — attesting to a boundary the RP controls, providing no transport security. Use **TDX aTLS** for any validator-as-host surface.

**Intel TDX / AMD SEV-SNP — assembly-first (VM-TEE).**
- Bind the channel pubkey-hash into the 64-byte `REPORTDATA` (TDX, then DCAP-quoted by the TD Quoting Enclave) / `REPORT_DATA` (SEV-SNP, VCEK-signed: ARK→ASK→VCEK). Establish aTLS via **Constellation / Contrast** (confidential-K8s; embed the CVM statement in the node cert), or release keys via **Confidential Containers / Trustee KBS** (attest → verify at the Attestation Service → release). (Constellation/Contrast *Attestation*; CoCo Trustee KBS; AMD *SEV-SNP Attestation*.)

**Sei / Tide profile fit.** Harbor-hosted agent runtimes + bridges → **AWS Nitro**, where the idiomatic "attested channel" is the **KMS-release pattern** above (`tee-profile.md` §6; `kit-aws-nitro.md` §6). Any surface that must defend against **validator-as-host** is the wrong fit for Nitro's host-trust model (`tee-profile.md` §1) → **Intel TDX**, with aTLS via the assembly-first path.

**Template-fit note:** §6 normally describes an Attester's *secret-release* pattern. Here it is the **channel-establishment** pattern across platforms — which on Nitro subsumes secret-release (KMS), and on SGX is the RA-TLS library flow; attester-side measurement/cert detail is redirected to the attester kits.
