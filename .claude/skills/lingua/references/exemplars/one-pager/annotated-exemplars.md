# Annotated 1-pager exemplars — what good looks like, with provenance

> **Corpus content (Design 03; harvested under PLT-491, verified 2026-06-12).** Positive exemplars only.
> Cite an entry as `cite: one-pager/exemplar/<slug>` → the `##` anchors below. **The slugs are stable
> cite targets — renaming one is a breaking change for citing rules.** Inclusion bar: real, verifiable,
> publicly accessible artifacts or doctrine — author-published or official; two famous candidates were
> excluded for failing it (a memo public only as a court exhibit; a memo whose full text was never
> verifiably published). Entries are annotated in our own words, keyed to `one-pager/shape/<anchor>`
> sections and audience-model rules (R1–R5). Nothing is reproduced beyond nominative titles and section
> names; license classes in `../../sources.md`. PD = public domain (17 U.S.C. §105; contractor and
> state-government works excepted; embedded third-party material stays copyrighted).
>

## ar-25-50-army-correspondence

**AR 25-50, "Preparing and Managing Correspondence"** — armypubs.army.mil (2020, admin rev. 2024) · License class: **public domain (§105)**.

- The BLUF mandate as regulation, not advice: the Army writing standard requires the main point at the
  beginning and active voice (¶1-38) — R2 made compulsory for an entire institution
  (→ `one-pager/shape/bluf-lead`).
- Its decision-memorandum format (¶2-8) types the body — purpose, recommendations, discussion,
  coordination — with a two-page ceiling: the genre's section functions named, not inferred
  (→ `one-pager/shape/purpose-and-ask`, `one-pager/shape/coordination-record`).
- Plain-writing mechanics carried as numbered rules (short words, ~15-word sentences, one-page default)
  — the page-discipline floor with measurable thresholds (→ `one-pager/shape/page-discipline`).

## secnav-m-5216-5-navy-manual

**SECNAV M-5216.5, "Department of the Navy Correspondence Manual"** — secnav.navy.mil (2015, ch-1 2018)
· License class: **public domain (§105)**.

- The action-memo format is the genre's tightest worked template: one-page limit, leading bullets
  carrying the action required and the due date, then typed RECOMMENDATION, COORDINATION (or "NONE"),
  Attachments, and Prepared-by lines (→ `one-pager/shape/purpose-and-ask`,
  `one-pager/shape/recommendation`).
- The decision memorandum types the decision onto the artifact — Approved / Disapproved / Other lines
  below the signature: the memo is its own decision record (→ `one-pager/shape/decision-block`).
- Coordination is treated as load-bearing: an action memo's credibility is tied to its recorded
  coordination — dissent and concurrence belong on the page (→ `one-pager/shape/coordination-record`).

## tongue-and-quill

**AFH 33-337, "The Tongue and Quill"** — e-publishing.af.mil (2015 ed.) · License class: **public domain (§105)** — embedded literary
epigraphs are third-party copyrighted; harvest the doctrine, not the epigraphs.

- The richest single PD source on BLUF as a *method*: it names the organization patterns — BLUF, BLIND
  (Bottom Line, Impact, Next steps, Details), and the eSSS staffing spine (PURPOSE → BACKGROUND →
  DISCUSSION → VIEWS OF OTHERS → RECOMMENDATION) (→ `one-pager/shape/bluf-lead`,
  `one-pager/shape/discussion-and-options`).
- Documents the genre's own exception: indirect order for audiences that will resist the conclusion —
  the convention ships with its boundary condition written down
  (→ `one-pager/shape/audience-exception`).

## omb-m-11-15

**OMB M-11-15, "Final Guidance on Implementing the Plain Writing Act of 2010"** —
obamawhitehouse.archives.gov (2011) · License class: **public domain (§105)**.

- Double-duty entry: it directs agencies to the Federal Plain Language Guidelines (purpose and bottom
  line first; background toward the end) *and is itself* a clean specimen of the executive memo genre —
  MEMORANDUM FOR / FROM / SUBJECT header, purpose-first opening paragraph
  (→ `one-pager/shape/bluf-lead`, `one-pager/shape/background-in-brief`).
- Provenance note: plainlanguage.gov now redirects to digital.gov; the 2011 guidelines text lives in
  the GSA archive — cite the archived text for the original wording.

## bvp-memo-anthology

**Bessemer Venture Partners' published investment memos** — bvp.com/memos · License class: **Reserved** (freely published;
cite-and-link only).

- Nineteen real internal memos (Shopify, Twilio, Pinterest, LinkedIn…) with a stable section spine
  ending in an explicit decision frame (Deal → Outcomes Analysis → Conclusion) — decision-memo
  discipline practiced at length, by an institution, on real stakes
  (→ `one-pager/shape/recommendation`, `one-pager/shape/decision-block`).
- The republished memos carry retrospective marginal annotations contrasting the original reasoning with
  what actually happened — the rare positive exemplar that grades its own past decisions in public.

## coinbase-mission-memo

**"Coinbase is a mission focused company"** (Brian Armstrong) — coinbase.com/blog (2020, with the
follow-up post) · License class: **Reserved** (author-published; cite-and-link only).

- A real CEO decision memo published verbatim: position stated first, then explicit
  what-we-will-do / what-we-won't-do enumeration — the BLUF + typed-commitments pattern outside
  government (→ `one-pager/shape/bluf-lead`, `one-pager/shape/recommendation`).
- The follow-up post executes the decision (the opt-out offer), closing the memo → action →
  accountability loop in public (→ `one-pager/shape/decision-block`).

## strom-awn-bluf

**"Bottom Line Up Front: write to make decisions faster"** (Matt Ström-Awn) — mattstromawn.com ·
License class: **Reserved** (cite-and-link only).

- The strongest practitioner treatment of BLUF as *decision* writing: traces the doctrine to its primary
  sources (AR 25-50, *The Tongue and Quill*) and draws the load-bearing distinction that the lead must
  carry the decision itself rather than recap the analysis (→ `one-pager/shape/bluf-lead`).
- Names BLUF's failure conditions (skeptical audiences, low sender credibility) — a practitioner source
  that documents the exception alongside the rule (→ `one-pager/shape/audience-exception`).

## sehgal-hbr-military-precision

**"How to Write Email with Military Precision"** (Kabir Sehgal) — hbr.org (2016) · License class: **Reserved**
(cite-and-link only).

- The mainstream-business import of the doctrine: typed subject-line keywords declaring the ask
  (INFO / REQUEST / ACTION) and a BLUF opening with background demoted to bullets — header-level typing
  of intent that an agent reader can route on (→ `one-pager/shape/purpose-and-ask`).

## smart-brevity

**_Smart Brevity_** (VandeHei, Allen, Schwartz) — Workman, 2022 · License class: **Reserved** — trade book
(cite-and-link only).

- The Axios codification of BLUF-descended executive writing: open with the one essential point, then
  scaffold the body with bolded, function-labeled signposts that type each paragraph's role — section
  typing by function, legible to a scanning human and a parsing agent alike
  (→ `one-pager/shape/bluf-lead`; R1 applied to memo scaffolding).
