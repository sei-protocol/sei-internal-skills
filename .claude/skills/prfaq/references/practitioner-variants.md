# Practitioner variants and optional additions

The Bryar/Carr canonical shape is the foundation. Modern practitioners outside Amazon have codified extensions and refinements. The skill recognizes both — the canonical core, plus a curated set of opt-in additions that don't bloat the document.

## Three templates compared

The three templates with explicit public structure are McAllister's, Bryar/Carr's (workingbackwards.com), and Calbucci's (theprfaq.com).

| Element | McAllister | Bryar/Carr | Calbucci |
|---|---|---|---|
| Title formula | `[COMPANY] ANNOUNCES [X] TO ENABLE [Y] TO [Z]` (explicit) | Implicit | Implicit |
| Customer quote | Fictional, required | Hypothetical, required | Fictional, before/after framing |
| Spokesperson quote | Required | Required | Implicit |
| PR length | ≤ ½ page | ≤ 1 page | 1 page (~7 paragraphs) |
| External FAQ count | — | — | 3–8 |
| Internal FAQ count | — | ~20+ | 12–18 |
| Whole doc | — | ~6 pages (book) | ≤ 6 pages (hard) |
| First-draft time-box | — | A few hours, not a few days | Iterative |
| Drafting order | PR-first | PR-first | FAQ-first (debugging mode) |

**Strongest shared elements:**
- Title formula `[COMPANY] ANNOUNCES [X] TO ENABLE [Y] TO [Z]`
- Mandatory fictional/hypothetical customer quote
- Problem-then-solution order
- Internal FAQ that interrogates business viability and competition

## Eight areas of practitioner disagreement

The highest-signal section — where the methodology gets bent.

### 1. Strict page count vs. principled brevity
Calbucci: 6 pages, hard ceiling. Bryar/Carr (public): clarity-first, no explicit count; the book lands ≤ 6. McAllister: PR alone ≤ ½ page. Cedric Chin: "the real work occurs in the accompanying FAQ sections," letting the FAQ grow until assumptions are falsified.
**Consensus:** ≤ 6 pages total, ≤ 1 page PR; the FAQ ceiling bends.

### 2. Who is the customer quote from?
McAllister and Calbucci: explicit fictional ("but one that sounds like it could be real"); Calbucci wants a before/after framing. Bryar/Carr: "hypothetical," sometimes drafted to an internal archetype ("Mary the customer").
**Disagreement:** named/specific persona vs. generic voice. Named-persona school is more common at non-Amazon companies adopting the method.
**Skill's choice:** named, situated; flat is more credible than enthusiastic; the 5-question test from `voice-discipline.md` is the gate.

### 3. PR-first or FAQ-first drafting?
McAllister and canonical Bryar/Carr: PR first, then FAQ. Calbucci's starting guide inverts: Internal FAQs first (customer/problem/market/value), then Customer FAQs (solution/feasibility/viability), then collapse the synthesis into the PR.
**Skill's choice:** PR-first when the customer thesis is settled (Bryar/Carr canonical), FAQ-first when uncertain (Calbucci's debug mode). When in doubt, FAQ-first.
**Source:** https://www.theprfaq.com/articles/prfaq-starting-guide

### 4. The "Alternatives Considered" subsection
- Bryar/Carr embed alternatives in the solution paragraph itself: "Today, customers with this problem use x, y, or z products..."
- Many practitioners add a standalone "Alternatives Considered" question to the internal FAQ — to surface the build/buy/partner/punt analysis.
- **This is the most common common-sense addition outside the canon.**

### 5. Tenets section
- Bryar/Carr discuss "tenets" as an Amazon leadership-principles concept, but tenets are not part of the canonical PR/FAQ structure.
- A growing practitioner habit at non-Amazon companies (Atlassian-style, GitLab handbooks) is to add a "Tenets" or "Principles" subsection to the internal FAQ.
- Legitimate addition that doesn't bloat the document — provided the tenets are falsifiable trade-offs ("we will X even at the cost of Y"), not platitudes ("be customer-obsessed").

### 6. Iteration count and rituals
- Bryar/Carr: reviewed in widening "concentric circles" of stakeholders; final round in front of senior leadership.
- Calbucci: "iterative" without prescription.
- McAllister: "keep iterating until they've come up with benefits that actually sound like benefits" — no count.
- Cedric Chin: at early stages, run *competing* PR variants (multiple PRs, no FAQ); at later stages, switch to FAQ-only documents addressing specific settled questions. "Don't let the process drive you."
- Julian Dunn pins down the review-meeting clock: silent read 10–12 min, discussion 20–30 min.

### 7. Calbucci's correction on "customer obsession"
He pushes back on "Customer Obsession" as the sole lens: PRFAQs that ignore business viability or team feasibility "lead to poor results or to a revolt." Bryar/Carr's public framing is customer-first heavy; Calbucci's correction is that the FAQ exists to interrogate viability, not decorate the PR. He also pushes back on "backwards" terminology: "the actual meaning is to *discover the desired outcome first*."

### 8. Naming the document
Bryar/Carr: PR/FAQ (slash). Calbucci: PRFAQ (no punctuation, now de facto). Cosmetic but signals lineage.

## Eleven common-sense additions outside the canon

Refinements that appear in multiple credible practitioner playbooks but are *not* in the Bryar/Carr canonical structure. The skill allows teams to opt-in.

1. **"Alternatives Considered" as a named internal-FAQ question.** Bryar/Carr embed it in the solution paragraph; experienced reviewers ask for an explicit build/buy/partner/punt section in the FAQ.
2. **"Tenets" / "Principles" subsection** in the internal FAQ — falsifiable trade-offs, not platitudes.
3. **Explicit FAQ-to-PR length ratio.** Several practitioners treat FAQ as ~3–5× the PR length. The canon doesn't enforce a ratio.
4. **Named customer personas in the quote.** "Mary, age 34, ..." rather than a generic customer. Common outside Amazon.
5. **Pre-mortem / "How could this fail?" question** as a required internal-FAQ entry. Calbucci, Hustle Badger, Reforge call this out explicitly; canonical Bryar/Carr discuss it qualitatively but don't enforce it as a question.
6. **Time-boxed first draft** (~4 hours / one sitting). Echoed by Bryar/Carr ("a few hours, not a few days") and codified into rituals by practitioners.
7. **Two-quote rule** (one customer, one spokesperson). Implicit in Bryar/Carr, explicit in McAllister.
8. **"What does success look like?" question** in the internal FAQ — named metrics with falsification thresholds. Canonical template implies it; modern practitioners require it explicitly. (Required by this skill — see FM23.)
9. **FAQ-first drafting as a debugging mode** (Calbucci): if the PR keeps changing, draft the FAQ to expose unstable assumptions, then redraft the PR last.
10. **Inversion exercise — write the *failure* press release** as a complement to the canonical one. Surfaces what flat-on-paper feels like (FM27 diagnostic).
11. **Codified review-meeting timing** (Julian Dunn): 10–12 min silent read, 20–30 min discussion.

The skill defaults to the canonical Bryar/Carr shape and allows teams to opt-in to any of these additions when the situation warrants — but never as a substitute for the canonical PR + external FAQ + internal FAQ structure.
