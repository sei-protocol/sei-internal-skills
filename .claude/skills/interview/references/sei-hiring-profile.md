# Sei hiring-bar profile (always-first overlay)

Loaded before scoring. It encodes what **Sei** weights and can **override the kit's generic anchors** in either direction — the same way a repo profile overrides `/idiomatic`'s generic rules. This is a **starting profile the hiring team refines**; where it is silent, fall back to the kit + `method.md`. Treat every line here as the team's stated bar, not external doctrine.

## What Sei weights (the through-line)

The bar is **logical, maintainable code, with real test coverage and documentation** — and, in the live discussion, the ability to **explore and defend the trade-offs of productionizing the system**. The take-home is built in a vacuum; we hire for the engineer who can take it from "works on my machine" to "holds up in production," so:

- **Dimensions 2, 4, 5 (maintainability, testing, documentation) are table stakes** — a clever-but-unmaintainable or untested sample is *not* at bar regardless of how impressive the core trick is.
- **Dimension 7 (production / operational thinking) is the senior separator** and the north star for the live discussion. We care less about a perfect take-home than about whether they can reason about scale, failure, and operability when pushed.
- **Dimension 3 (trade-off articulation)** is weighted heavily — Sei wants engineers who can say *why* they chose a structure for *this* workload, not just that it works.
- **Dimension 8 (AI-collaboration)** matters because we *give* candidates AI: we want engineers who scale themselves with it — decompose, verify, and override — not who paste and hope. Reading the candidate's mental model for AI is an explicit goal, not an afterthought.

## Level targets

- **3 = the hire bar** on a dimension; **4 = bar-raising.** A solid hire is a row of 3s with sound reasoning.
- **L4 / L5 (solid IC):** correct, clean, well-tested solution to the problem **as posed**; trade-offs articulated within the task.
- **L6 (senior / staff):** treats the prompt as a design problem — reframes scope, surfaces compounding trade-offs, builds for production beyond what was asked. (Dimensions 3, 6, 7 at 4.)

Calibrate the *recommendation* to the role's target level: a strong-L4 sample is a **hire** for an L4 req and a **lean-no** for an L6 req — same artifact, different bar. State the target level in the lead.

## Evaluate the artifact, not the clock

We tell candidates a rough time box, but **we score the work, not the hours spent.** Do not reward or penalize a submission for apparent time investment, and do not infer effort from polish. A tight, well-reasoned 90-minute solution that states what it deliberately cut can out-score a sprawling one. (The candidate-facing prompt's time framing is a separate concern owned by the hiring team; this profile governs how the *artifact* is judged.)

## Fairness

- Same anchors for every candidate; cite the rubric, never a gut feel.
- A genuinely close call is flagged for human calibration, not forced into a confident number.
- Score what the prompt asked; the absence of un-asked scope is a discussion vertical, not a low score.
- This is a **signal for the human**, who owns the hire/level decision.
